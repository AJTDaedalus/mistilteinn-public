## 1. The blocker this removes

`src/app/sim/search.ts` is a working optimizer that nothing imports, and the reason is one
field. `SearchContext` needs a `hit` — a multiplier, a damage channel, a scaling stat — and
`HitSpec` says why there is none:

> Deriving a talent's multiplier, scaling stat and damage channel from a character's stored
> talent rows is the kit corpus's job (Chunk H), and this repo has no kit corpus. Rather than
> parse a multiplier out of a `desc` string and present the result as though it came from the
> game, the caller states the hit. `TODO(chunk-H)`: a `talent` field that resolves this from
> the build's own character replaces it.

Chunk H is closed, so that `TODO` names a chunk that will not arrive. The caller stating the
hit means a user typing `1.2091`, which makes every damage surface unusable by anyone not
already reading a spreadsheet.

**Owner decision, 2026-08-24: parse the seeded talent tables.** And: **explain before
optimize** — one build's damage, with its derivation, before a ranking of ten thousand.

## 2. The data supports it, and this was checked

Every figure below is counted from `tests/fixtures/nanoka_gi/detail/character_*.json`, not
recalled.

A talent's `promote[level]` carries `desc` — a padded list of `"Label|{paramN:FMT}"` — beside
a flat `param` list that `paramN` indexes 1-based:

```
"desc":  ["Cutting DMG|{param1:P}", "Bloom DMG|{param2:P}", "CD|{param4:F1}s",
          "Energy Cost|{param5:I}", "", "", …]
"param": [1.123, 1.6845, 5, 20, 80, 0, 0, …]
```

Across **498 non-empty rows** there are exactly four format codes — `F1P` (225), `P` (165),
`F1` (123), `I` (30) — and five tails: `s`, `/s`, `×3`, `+`, `/`.

**The params are already fractions.** `1.123` is 112.3%, matching this repo's standing
convention. `P` and `F1P` differ only in display precision, so neither needs converting.

### 2.1 Three row shapes, and the separator distinguishes them

Seven distinct rows across the fixtures use more than one param or a repeat:

| shape | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `×N` | `4-Hit DMG\|{param4:F1P}×3` | one hit, landing three times |
| `A+B` | `Charged Attack DMG\|{param6:F1P}+{param7:P}` | two parts of one attack, **summed** |
| `A/B` | `Low/High Plunge DMG\|{param10:P}/{param11:P}` | two **alternatives**, not a sum |

`+` and `/` mean different things and the parser must not treat them alike. Summing a low and
high plunge would produce a multiplier for a hit nobody can perform — a plausible number for
an impossible action, which is this repo's characteristic failure.

### 2.2 What makes a row a damage row

Label contains `DMG`. The alternative — trusting the `P` format — is wrong: `Blazing
Threshold` and `Nightsoul Point Limit` are percentages that are not damage. The non-DMG labels
in the fixtures are `CD`, `Duration`, `Energy Cost`, `Stamina Drain`, `Activation Stamina
Consumption`, `Charged Attack Stamina Cost`, `Infusion Duration`, and two thresholds.

**This is a label heuristic over English text, and that is a real limitation** — the fixtures
are the `en` locale. It is recorded as such rather than hidden, and M.4 is what stops it being
taken on trust.

### 2.3 A second thing it closes

`Energy Cost|{param5:I}` is where `Character.burst_energy_cost` comes from — nullable to this
day because, per `catalogue.py`, it is *"nullable until the burst's `Energy Cost` row is
parsed out of its description"*. The same parser fills it.

## 3. Why this is citing rather than inventing

`backend/CLAUDE.md` anticipated it:

> Talent multipliers are kept as published tables, one row per level, with the `desc` format
> strings beside the `param` list. Parsing them is Chunk F's job; **a mis-parse there is then
> a bug in a parser rather than data that was lost on import.**

The table *is* the citation. The risk is a mis-parse, and a mis-parse is checkable.

## 4. And it is checkable against gcsim

gcsim's per-character data files are **generated** — `zz_kamisatoayaka.dm.go` opens with
`Code generated by "pipeline"; DO NOT EDIT` — and carry the same numbers, per level, with the
source row named in a comment:

```go
// burst: KamisatoArtSoumetsu - Cutting DMG: {0}
burstCut = []float64{ 1.123, 1.207225, 1.29145, … }
```

`1.123` is exactly what nanoka publishes for Ayaka's `Cutting DMG` at level 1. So the parse
can be **crossvalidated against an independent implementation** rather than merely reviewed —
the same discipline `tests/crossval` applies to the damage chain, applied to the kit data that
chain reads.

That is M.4, and it is what makes §2.2's English-label heuristic safe to rely on: a heuristic
that agrees with gcsim across a hundred characters is not resting on the heuristic any more.

## 5. Approach

- **M.1 — the parser.** `desc` + `param` → structured hits. A pure function over two lists,
  testable without a session, exhaustively exercised against the committed fixtures.
- **M.2 — expose it.** `HitSpec` gains the `talent` field its `TODO` names, resolving a
  multiplier from the build's own character and talent level. The explicit `multiplier` stays,
  because a caller who wants to ask about a hypothetical hit should still be able to.
- **M.3 — the explain page.** Pick a build, pick a talent and which hit, see the number **and
  the multipliers that produced it**, which `/builds/{id}/explain` already returns and nothing
  displays.
- **M.4 — crossval against gcsim.** A gate comparing parsed multipliers with gcsim's generated
  tables, over the characters both implement, **reporting its own coverage** — the shape
  `test_gcsim_character_coverage.py` established.

## 6. Testing strategy

| task | what proves it |
|---|---|
| M.1 | every row shape from §2.1 parses to the right arity; `+` sums and `/` does not; a `DMG` label with no param, and a param index past the end of `param`, are refused rather than defaulted |
| M.2 | a build with a talent level resolves the level's row, not level 1; an unimplemented character refuses with its reason |
| M.3 | the derivation is shown, not just the total; a failed load surfaced |
| M.4 | disagreement with gcsim **fails**; coverage is reported, and a run that compared nothing is a failure rather than a pass |

That last line is the standing rule: a skipped check blocks. A crossval that silently matched
zero characters is exactly the green-by-absence failure fairy shipped.

## 7. Non-goals

- **No constellation or passive effects on the multiplier.** Those are kit modelling, and
  `weapon_comparison.unmodelled_passives` already exists to say when a result does not include
  them. Nothing here changes what the damage chain knows.
- **No reaction or infusion inference.** The channel comes from the character's element or the
  label; anything cleverer is guessing.
- **No optimizer UI.** Next chunk, and this is what unblocks it.
- **No non-English locale support.** §2.2's heuristic is `en`-only and recorded as such.

## 8. Open questions

- **OQ-M1 — what does a `/` row resolve to?** *Answered: both, as separate hits.* A caller
  picks one. Collapsing them loses a hit; summing them invents one.
- **OQ-M2 — does `×3` return one hit or three?** *Answered: one hit, with a `hits` count.* The
  damage chain multiplies a per-hit figure by crit and DEF terms, and `param × 3` pre-multiplied
  would apply those once to a tripled number. Keeping the count separate lets the caller decide.
- **OQ-M3 — what happens when gcsim and nanoka disagree in M.4?** *Not answered here.* It has
  not happened yet and inventing a policy for an unobserved disagreement is how a tolerance
  gets set to hide one. The first real case decides, and it gets recorded in
  `docs/CROSSVAL_gcsim.md` beside the two divergences already there.
